How this works

This webapp is a side project of Christopher Williams, PhD. While he has tested it against synthetic data, it's important to remember, the webapp is still in beta testing and therefore may not do exactly what you want or expect it to do. See below for an explanation of what is going on under the hood.

Your data never leaves this tab

The file you upload is parsed and processed entirely in your browser. Nothing is sent to a server, because there isn't one. Refreshing or closing the page clears everything.

The weighting method

Weights come from raking (iterative proportional fitting): each active variable is adjusted in turn to match its target proportions, cycling through all active variables repeatedly. This stops once the largest single adjustment in a full pass drops below 0.05%, or after 50 passes, whichever comes first.

Stratified samples

Turning on "Sample design" doesn't just add another raking margin. It sets each respondent's starting weight to population share ÷ sample share for their stratum, and raking then adjusts from that starting point rather than from 1. This is the standard approach for a design that sampled strata at different rates.

Trimming

If weight capping is on, weights outside your min/max are pulled back to the bound after each full pass, then everything is rescaled so the average weight is still exactly 1.

Comparing against Stata, R, or another tool

Margins are raked sequentially (i.e., one variable at a time). While this is standard, it means the exact path to convergence depends on iteration order and stopping rule. Expect agreement to a few hundredths of a percentage point against another correctly-specified raking run, not bit-for-bit identical weights, unless the tolerance and variable order are matched exactly.
